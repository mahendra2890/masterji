# Playbook: Writing the Post
*(inspired by Harry Dry's Marketing Examples and its copywriting teardowns —
Masterji's own distillation)*

## The post is not the hard part; posting it is
`launch-checklist.md` tells you **where** to post and never how to write it.
So the draft sits in your notes for a third day, and you tell yourself you
are polishing. You are not. This is the first thing you will publish under
your own name in front of people who know you, and the fear is of writing it
badly in front of them.

Good news: the post is mostly already written. It is sitting in your
VALIDATION notes.

## Open with their problem, in their words
`customer-conversations.md` promised that their words become your landing
page. This is where you cash that. Go back to what the people you
interviewed actually said and open with the sentence one of them used —
"I miss dinner every time a lab runs late" beats anything you would write
about "streamlining hostel food logistics".

If you open with what your product *is*, the reader has to do the work of
recognising themselves. If you open with what they said, they recognise
themselves in one line and keep reading.

## One post, one room
Write for the 340 people in the group you are posting into, not for "the
public". A post for everybody has to stay vague to stay true, and vague is
what gets scrolled past. Name the room in the post if it fits: your hostel,
your campus, the sellers in that WhatsApp group. Somebody who is in it will
feel it.

Posting the same paragraph into four rooms is four half-posts. Write the
one for the room most likely to answer, and rewrite it for the next room
when you get there.

## Concrete beats clever
Every clever line you are proud of is costing you a specific one. Give the
reader something they can picture:
- a number ("I talked to 11 hostellers; 9 had skipped dinner that week"),
- a screenshot of the thing working,
- the phone-shot demo — thirty seconds, your voice, your actual screen.
`launch-checklist.md` already prefers that to a polished page, and it is
right: a rough demo of a real thing outperforms a beautiful page about an
idea.

No taglines. No "revolutionising". If a sentence could sit on any other
product's post, cut it.

## Post as a person, not as a company
"We are excited to announce" is a company nobody has heard of clearing its
throat. Write "I built this because —" and finish the sentence honestly.
Being a student building this for your own hostel is not a weakness to hide;
it is the most credible thing about the post. And write it in the register
the room actually speaks — Hinglish where the room is Hinglish. A room
switching into formal English to be sold to notices.

## End with the smallest ask you can cost
Do not end with "let me know what you think" — that costs the reader
nothing and tells you nothing. End with one ask that has a price on it,
and make the price small: reply with your hostel block, send me your
order for Friday, ₹50 to hold a slot. A costed yes is the only kind that
means anything, and it is what LAUNCH is going to ask you for evidence of.
