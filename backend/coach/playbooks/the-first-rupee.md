# Playbook: The First Rupee
*(inspired by Rob Walling's "Start Small, Stay Small" and Patrick McKenzie's public pricing essays — Masterji's own distillation)*

## Why the ask is the hard part
The launch ladder says a ₹99 payment tells the truth. It does not say how to
get one. Deploying is not the hard part of this phase — asking a person you
have to see again for money is. For most builders here it is the first time
they have said a price out loud to someone who can say no to their face.

## Your price is already in your notes
You do not have to invent a number. Your VALIDATION notes say what these
people do today and what it costs them, and the workaround is the anchor.
Someone paying ₹210 for ₹90 of food because the mess shut at 22:00 has told
you what a bad night is worth to them. Price against that. Not against your
hosting bill, and not against what feels polite to ask a classmate.

## Never ask "would you pay?"
Same rule that governs the conversations: people are fantasists about the
future and honest about the present. "Would you pay ₹99 for this?" earns a
warm yes and an empty account. Put the UPI QR in front of them and ask for
the payment now. The ask is the test; what happens next is the data.

## Free is allowed, vague is not
Free with an end date and the price said out loud up front — "free this
month, ₹99 from the 1st" — is a launch. Free with "we'll figure out pricing
later" is you avoiding the sentence. Anyone who leaves when the price arrives
was never a user, and week one is a cheaper place to learn that than month six.

## "Too expensive" is the start of the conversation
It is not a no. Ask what they pay for the workaround today — that number is
the negotiation, and it is usually larger than what you were about to charge.
If the answer is that they genuinely have no money for this, you have learned
something true about the segment, which is worth more than the sale was.

## The first price is a hypothesis
Pick one, say it, watch what happens. ₹99 today. Revisit at user ten — not
mid-week, and not after one person winced. Moving the price every time
somebody hesitates teaches you nothing and tells the room you don't believe
the number either.

## What counts as PROOF for Masterji
The bar hasn't moved, and this playbook aims at two parts of it: what a
stranger actually did, with the number if there is one — who paid, how much,
for what — or a real rejection with the reason they gave. "They said they'd
pay" is neither of those.

## Signals you're hiding
- The thing has been live a week and no price has been said out loud.
- One more feature before you'd feel right charging for it.
- Discounting before anybody has objected to the price.
- Asking friends, who will pay out of kindness and teach you nothing.
