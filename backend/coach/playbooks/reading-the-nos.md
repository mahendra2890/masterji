# Playbook: Reading the Nos
*(inspired by Ash Maurya's "Running Lean" and its validate-then-pivot loop — Masterji's own distillation, not the book)*

## What this covers, and what it doesn't
The other VALIDATION playbooks are about getting conversations and having them
well. This is the pile of notes afterwards, and the question none of them
answers: what do these mean together, and do I keep going?

Read them before the week ends. A builder opening their notes for the first time
on the night they want to quit is reading them in the worst possible light.

## Sort by commitment, not enthusiasm
Put every person in one of two columns. Not "keen" and "not keen" — you cannot
measure that and neither can they.

- **Gave something up.** Time, an introduction, a look at their books, a next
  meeting, money. Anything that cost them.
- **Was polite.** Said it's a good idea. Said they'd probably use it. Said to
  send them a link.

The second column is not evidence — not even when it is long, and not even when
the people were sincere. The commitment is already the part of the bar Masterji
asks you for, and this is why: it is the only line in your notes the other
person had to pay for.

An empty first column after three conversations is the finding of the week. It
does not mean stop. It means the ask was too big, too vague, or aimed at the
wrong person, and the next conversation should test which.

## The pattern bar
One person's problem is a story. A pattern is what you can act on:

- **Three people naming the same workaround is a segment.** Not the same
  complaint — the same *workaround*. What people already do about a problem is
  the truest measure of what it costs them.
- **Five people with five different complaints is not one yet.** That is five
  problems, and it is the commonest shape of a "validated" idea that dies in
  BUILD.
- **Nobody has done anything about it** means real but not urgent, and people do
  not pay, switch or return for a problem they never tried to solve.

## Pivot the who before the what
When the notes are thin, the reflex is to change the idea. Change the segment
first. The same product aimed at people who feel the problem twice a week
instead of twice a term is a different business with the same code behind it.
Narrowing — from "students" to "second-year hostellers whose labs run past
21:00" — is the cheapest move you have, and usually the one that was missing.
Only when the who has changed and the answers are still flat has the what earned
a look.

## The honest kill
Sometimes the idea is wrong, and closing it is not a failure.

Masterji does not take your word for how it went — it reads the record. An idea
dropped before anyone was asked reads as **UNTESTED**. An idea taken to real
people who said no reads as **INVALIDATED**, and that needs at least two
accepted proofs from VALIDATION onward. That label is not a consolation prize:
it is the difference between "it didn't work out" and "I found out, and here is
what I found", and nobody gets it without having done the work.

Never make that call on one bad night. One rude no, one cancelled meeting, one
person who didn't get it — none of that is data, and all of it arrives at 11pm
feeling like a verdict. Decide on the week's notes in the morning.

## What counts as PROOF for Masterji
Nothing here changes the bar, and nothing here can be filed on its own. Deciding
to persist is not an evening's proof; deciding to pivot is not either. The proof
is still the conversation.

And there is no substitute for it. A landing page with a signup counter, an
Instagram poll, a form nobody filled in — none of these is a conversation, and
this phase's bar is conversation-shaped on purpose. A smoke test gives you a
click rate. It cannot tell you what somebody already tried and why it failed
them, which is the only thing that tells you what to build.

## Signals you're hiding
- Every note is in the "was polite" column and you have called it validation.
- Seven conversations in, you cannot name one workaround two of them share.
- You changed the idea after a single bad conversation.
- You are planning the next version instead of booking the next conversation.
- You would rather put up a landing page than ask one person for an hour.
