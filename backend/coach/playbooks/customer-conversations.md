# Playbook: Customer Conversations
*(inspired by Rob Fitzpatrick's "The Mom Test" — this is Masterji's own distillation, not the book)*

## Why this is the only work that counts in VALIDATION
Your idea is a guess. Code doesn't test a guess; conversations with people who
have the problem do. Every day spent "preparing to build" before three real
conversations is a day spent hiding.

## The three rules
1. **Talk about their life, not your idea.** The moment you pitch, people get
   polite. Politeness is poison — everyone's mother says the idea is lovely.
   Ask about what they already do; never ask "would you use this?"
2. **Ask about specifics in the past, not opinions about the future.**
   "When did this last happen? What did it cost you? What did you try?"
   beats "would you pay for X?" every time. People are honest about what they
   did and fantasists about what they'll do.
3. **Dig for commitment, not compliments.** A good conversation ends with the
   other person giving something up: time (another meeting booked), reputation
   (an intro to a colleague with the same problem), or money (a pre-order,
   a deposit). "Sounds great, keep me posted" is a rejection wearing a smile.

## What a conversation looks like
- Find them where they already are: hostel groups, WhatsApp communities,
  college E-Cells, the shop down the road. Ten minutes is plenty.
- Open with their world: "You run deliveries in this area — what's the most
  annoying part of collecting payments?"
- Shut up and listen. Note exact phrases. Their words become your landing page.
- End with an ask that costs them something: "Can I come back Thursday and
  watch you do this?" / "Who else has this problem — can you intro me?"

## What counts as PROOF for Masterji
Notes from a real conversation with a real person: who they were, three
specific things they said (their words), what they last did about the problem,
and what commitment you asked for — plus whether you got it. A voice note or
photo of your notebook is fine. A survey is not a conversation. A friend
being nice is not a customer.

## Signals you're learning
- You heard something that surprised you or contradicted your plan.
- You can name what the person does TODAY instead of using your product.
- Someone gave up time, reputation or money.

## Signals you're hiding
- "I'm building a survey first." A survey is a way to avoid ten uncomfortable
  minutes. Go have the minutes.
- Only talking to friends who'll be kind.
- Rewriting the pitch instead of booking the next conversation.
