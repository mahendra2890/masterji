# Playbook: Coming Back
*(inspired by Andrew Chen's "The Cold Start Problem" and his writing on
retention and the leaky bucket — Masterji's own distillation)*

## The only number in this phase is a person's name
This phase asks for one stranger who came back on their own, or who paid.
Every other number lies to you at this size. Ten signups with nobody
returning is a worse week than one signup and one return, and a product
that keeps acquiring to cover people leaving is a bucket with a hole in
it — you are working to stay level.

At ten users you cannot measure retention, and you do not need to. You
need to be able to say a name. If you cannot tell whether the same person
came back or a second person arrived for the first time, that is the
thing to fix tonight, and the fix is usually a notebook rather than
analytics.

## A return is caused by last time, not by this time
The reason somebody comes back is not a feature you shipped this week. It
is what happened the last time they used it: something got done, faster
or with less mess than their old way, and they noticed. If the first use
did not do that, no amount of polish this week brings them back.

So when you are asking "how do I get returns", the honest question is
about the visit you already had, not the one you want next: **what
happened to them last time, and was it worth the trip?**

## Coming back, and being reminded
These look identical in a count and are opposite in meaning.

- **Came back:** they thought of it themselves, or hit the problem again
  and reached for you. That is a product with a reason to exist.
- **Was reminded:** you messaged them, and they came. That tells you your
  message works. It says nothing about the product yet.

Both are worth having — over-serving your first ten by hand is the work,
and reaching out is part of it. What you must not do is count them as the
same thing on the record. If you nudged them, write that down beside the
return; a phase cleared on nudges you sent is a phase you will have to
clear again when you stop sending them.

## The one who came back is worth ten who signed up
Go and ask them. Not "did you like it" — ask about the actual trip:

- What were you doing right before you opened it?
- What would you have done if it hadn't existed?
- What nearly stopped you coming back?

The first question finds the trigger, which is the thing you can go and
find more of. The second prices you against the real alternative, which
is almost never a competitor and almost always a workaround. The third is
the one they will only answer if you ask plainly, and it is usually
small, dull and fixable this week.

One of these conversations beats a week of guessing, and it is the same
interview VALIDATION taught, pointed at somebody who has now actually
used the thing.

## What not to reach for
Notifications, streaks, badges, points, a referral loop. Every one of
them is a way to manufacture the *look* of a return without the reason
for one, and at ten users they hide the only signal you have. Nothing on
that list is why anybody comes back to anything they actually value; it
is what gets added later to a product that already works. If the honest
answer is that nobody has a reason to return yet, that is a real finding
about the idea, and it belongs on the record rather than under a push
notification.

## What counts as PROOF for Masterji
- The same person, back a second time, with what they did and when — and
  say plainly whether anybody prompted them.
- Or a payment: who, how much, what for.
- The conversation with a returning user is not itself a proof of this
  phase's bar, but what it turns up is usually the next evening's work.
