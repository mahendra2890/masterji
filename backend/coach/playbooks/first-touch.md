# Playbook: The First Touch
*(inspired by Steve Blank's "The Four Steps to the Epiphany" and its get-out-of-the-building discipline — Masterji's own distillation, not the book)*

## What this covers, and what it doesn't
The other BUILD playbooks are about the thing you are making: what to cut, what
not to over-build, how to ship every day. This is the other half of the phase's
bar. BUILD does not end when the thing runs — it ends when a real person has
touched it, and that is a different job with a different fear attached.

Start it on the first day of BUILD. The person is harder to get than the
feature is to finish.

## You already have the list
You are not looking for users. You are looking for the people you spoke to in
VALIDATION, and their names are in your own notes. "I asked you about ___ last
month. I built the smallest version. Can I show you for ten minutes?" — that
message has a hit rate nothing cold will match, because it is the second half
of a conversation they already agreed to have.

If VALIDATION's notes are too thin to pull a name from, that is the finding: go
get one more conversation before you write another line of code.

## The smallest thing they can touch
Whatever is running tonight. A link works. A screen share works. Your phone,
unlocked, handed across a table, works — and it is usually the fastest, because
it needs no deploy and no account.

It does not need to be finished, styled, or safe from crashing in front of you.
It needs to exist and to be reachable by someone who is not you. A polished demo
next week is worth less than a rough one tonight: the point is not to impress
them, it is to find out what they do.

## Watch, do not demo
This is the whole skill, and almost nobody does it the first time.

- **Give them a task, not a tour.** "Here — order tonight's dinner." Then stop
  talking. The urge to narrate is enormous and it destroys the evidence.
- **Silence is the data.** When they pause, that is a finding. Jump in to
  explain and you have deleted it.
- **Do not defend anything.** "That's meant to be —" is the sentence to
  swallow. They are not wrong about their own confusion.
- **Write down what they DID.** People are polite about software the same way
  they are polite about ideas. The click, the hesitation, the thing they never
  found — those cannot flatter you.
- **One question at the end.** "What would you have done if I weren't sitting
  here?" The honest answer is often "closed it".

## What counts as PROOF for Masterji
BUILD's bar is a link to something running, **or** evidence a real user touched
it — and the phase cannot be left without at least one evening of the second
kind.

"I showed Priya the app and she said it looks useful" is a demo you gave.
"Priya opened it Thursday at 21:40, ordered from Block C, and spent a minute
looking for the payment screen" is a touch. The difference is who was holding
the phone.

A user you had to recruit by hand still counts — nothing here asks for
strangers arriving on their own. You operating it while they watch does not.

## Signals you're hiding
- The demo is scheduled for "when it's ready", and the date has moved twice.
- You have built three more features since anyone but you opened it.
- You are polishing the screen you would show first, not the one they would
  reach second.
- You caught yourself saying "they wouldn't understand it yet".
- You would rather write the landing page than message someone from your own
  notes.
