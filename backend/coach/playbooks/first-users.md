# Playbook: The First Users, By Hand
*(inspired by Paul Graham's "Do Things That Don't Scale" and Eric Ries' pivot-or-persevere in "The Lean Startup" — Masterji's own distillation)*

## What this phase is, and where it stops
One stranger coming back on their own, or paying. That is the whole target.
Not ten thousand users, not a growth loop, not a funding round — those are
past what this coaching covers, and advice about them at this stage is a way
of not doing tonight's work. This ends at the first ten people who came back.

The post going out was not the finish. Almost everything that decides whether
this becomes real happens after it, and almost nobody is watching by then.

## The moves

**Recruit one at a time.** The launch ladder's first rung — a personal message
to somebody from your VALIDATION notes — is not a thing you did once on launch
night. It is the work, every evening, until ten people have used it. One
message to one person beats a post to four hundred, because you can read the
reply.

**Over-serve the first ten, embarrassingly.** Reply within the hour. Watch
them use it, in person if they are on your campus. Fix the thing that broke
for them tonight, and tell them you fixed it. This does not scale and it is
not supposed to: you are buying the one thing you cannot buy later, which is
somebody who will tell you the truth about your product.

**Stay the concierge.** Doing behind the curtain by hand what the product is
supposed to do automatically is still legitimate here. If matching orders to
payments means you sit with a sheet at 22:00, sit with the sheet. Automate the
step only once somebody has come back to it twice.

**Watch returns, not signups.** Signups are the number that flatters. The
number that means something is: did the same person come back without being
reminded? One returning user is the whole scoreboard. Ten signups and zero
returns is a worse week than one signup and one return, and if you cannot say
which of yours came back, that is the thing to go and find out tonight.

**Ask the ones who did not come back.** They are more useful than the ones who
did, and they will tell you if you ask about what they did instead — not
whether they liked it. This is the same question VALIDATION asked, pointed at
your own product.

**Then decide, on the week's notes.** Enough of them coming back means keep
going. Enough of them not, with the same reason, means the thing to change is
named for you. Neither is decided on one bad evening — that is mood, not
evidence.

## What counts as PROOF for Masterji
- The same person using it a second time without being asked: what they did,
  when, and that nobody prompted them. Two different people once each is not
  this.
- A payment: who paid, how much, and what for. A promise to pay is not a
  payment.

## Signals you're hiding
- Chasing new signups while nobody who signed up last week has come back.
- Building the feature the one returning user did not ask for.
- Reading about growth loops, ads or referral mechanics at ten users.
- "I'll ask for money once it's more polished." The price is a question, and
  it is answered by asking it.
