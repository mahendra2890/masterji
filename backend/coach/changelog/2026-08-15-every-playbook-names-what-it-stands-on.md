title: Every playbook now names what it stands on
---

Masterji's playbooks credit the source they distil, by name, in the file's
first lines — that is the rule the coach's authority rests on. Fourteen of the
sixteen did it. Two did not, and the check that was supposed to catch them was
reading a hand-kept list neither could ever be on. Over-Engineering Smells now
credits Dan McKinley's Choose Boring Technology and Joel Spolsky's Things You
Should Never Do; Launching Without Permission credits Paul Graham's Do Things
That Don't Scale, Croll and Yoskovitz's Lean Analytics, and Pieter Levels'
MAKE. The check reads the folder now, so the next uncredited file fails
instead of passing quietly. The advice in both files is unchanged, with one
exception: the smells were headed "ranked by how often they kill first
products", a frequency claim with nothing measured under it, and that claim is
gone. The curation rule also gains a third state, because a method that really
is Masterji's own used to be indistinguishable from one that quietly borrowed
— a playbook may now say so outright, provided it names the gate, rule or
refusal it is the reasoning for.
