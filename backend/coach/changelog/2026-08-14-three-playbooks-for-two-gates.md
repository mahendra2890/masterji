title: Three new playbooks, on the two gates nothing was teaching
---

Two phases were refusing you on conditions the coach had nothing to say about.
BUILD will not let you leave without evidence a real user touched the thing, and
all three of its playbooks were about building it — what to cut, what not to
over-engineer, how to ship daily. Not one was about getting a person in front of
it. VALIDATION started counting distinct people rather than evenings, which
quietly made *who* your first three are the whole question, and nothing covered
the case every first-time builder actually starts with: the friend who wants you
to succeed and will say yes to anything. So there are three new ones.

**The First Touch** (BUILD) — recruiting your first user by hand out of the
VALIDATION notes you already have, the smallest thing you can put in front of
them tonight, and how to watch instead of demo. It also draws the line the gate
draws: "I showed her and she said it looks useful" is a demo you gave; what she
did when she was holding the phone is a touch.

**Talking to People You Know** (VALIDATION) — why a friendly yes teaches you
nothing, what a friend is genuinely good for (the introduction, above all), the
framing that gets somebody talking about their own last Tuesday instead of
reviewing your idea, and when to stop mining your own corridor.

**Reading the Nos** (VALIDATION) — what a week of notes means together. Sorting
by what people gave up rather than how keen they sounded, the bar for calling
something a pattern, changing who you are building for before changing what you
are building, and the honest kill: an idea taken to real people who said no is
recorded as invalidated, which is validation working rather than failing.

Nothing about any gate moved. The same proofs cost the same, the counts are
unchanged, and no phase got easier or harder to leave — what changed is that the
two hardest bars now have something behind them. As always, you can read every
word the coach is working from: the whole corpus is in the public repository.
