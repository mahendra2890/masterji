title: The problem statement playbook asks for the three things the gate counts
---

The problem statement playbook taught four parts — who, the trigger, the
workaround, why the workaround fails — and then told you that a paragraph
missing any of them was an idea rather than a problem statement. The gate has
never asked for the trigger: IDEA counts who has the problem, what they do
about it today, and why that's bad. So a builder who read the playbook properly
could write everything Masterji actually asks for and still conclude they were
not ready to file. The playbook now names those three as the standard. The
trigger stays where it was, taught as method rather than as a requirement — the
moment the problem shows up, and how often, is still what tells you whether the
problem is worth acting on, and both of the accepted examples have one. Nothing
about the gate changed; it was already the more honest of the two.
