title: A playbook on narrowing the first user, so IDEA's "place" is answerable
---

IDEA asks you for one specific place these people already are, and it will not
take a channel — "Reddit" and "LinkedIn" are not rooms and do not count. Then it
asks why you believe they are there. Both of those questions sit downstream of a
cut most builders have never made: from "people like me" to a group with an
address. The corpus had nothing on that cut. **The Problem Statement** teaches
what a statement has to contain, **Choosing the One Idea** teaches how to pick
between candidates, and "be specific about the who" was the whole of the advice
in between — which tells you to cut without telling you where.

**Narrowing the First User** (IDEA) is the third playbook in the phase. Cut on
circumstance rather than attribute: "small businesses" is an attribute, and if a
person can match your description perfectly and still not have the problem, you
cut on the wrong axis. The Thursday test — name the room, then ask whether you
*could* be standing in it this Thursday. Could, not will: you are still not
messaging anybody in IDEA, and the question is only testing whether a door
exists. Why a group whose members have never met is a list rather than a
segment. And the part that reads backwards: narrowing *widens* the route, because
a "who" narrow enough to have an address answers "who else?" by itself, while a
wide one has no adjacency at all.

No gate moved. The proofs cost exactly what they cost this morning, IDEA still
opens on the same four parts, and nothing about the phase got harder to leave.
The coach simply has something to say now when the honest answer to "where are
they?" is "everywhere". As always, you can read every word he is working from —
the whole corpus is in the public repository.
