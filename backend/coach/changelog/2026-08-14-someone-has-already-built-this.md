title: A fourth way into the workshop, and the door now mentions the room

The workshop offered three openers — no idea yet, three ideas and can't pick, is
my idea too obvious — and was missing the one that stops more people than any of
them: **someone's already built this.** That is not the same worry as "too
obvious". Obvious is a fear that the idea isn't worth doing; this one is a
belief that the question is already settled, so there is no point starting. It
is also the one most often wrong, and Masterji now has a straight answer for it
rather than an improvised one.

The short version of that answer: a product that exists means somebody funded a
team to chase this problem, which is the best free evidence you will ever get
that the problem is real. What it is not is evidence that it's solved for the
people you have in mind. So the room asks you the two questions that actually
settle it — what the existing thing doesn't do for your people, and whether you
can name one of them using it badly, working around it, or refusing to pay. If
you can, that's your idea, sharper than it was a minute ago. If you can't, that
isn't a dead end either: it's the first conversation VALIDATION exists to make
you have, and you can have it this week. What you won't get is a reassurance
that the market is big enough for both, because nobody can act on that.

The landing page also stopped hiding the room. It described a ladder starting at
IDEA and never mentioned that there's a workshop under the commit box — so a
visitor whose honest position was "I don't have an idea yet" read the first rung
as a wall. The page now says the room is there, what it costs (fifteen turns),
and that nothing in it counts toward anything, which is the point of it.

Nothing about any gate moved. The workshop still banks nothing, still ends when
its turns do, and committing is still yours to press.
